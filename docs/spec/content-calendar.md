## 5.20 Content and community calendar

A Chatter-specific planning and approval workflow connecting LGBTQ+ community observances, winter/outdoor sports moments, heritage and social-justice dates, Chatter events, partner opportunities, and campaigns to practical content work — not a full social-media publishing suite, and not a replacement for event registration, email marketing, or a CRM. It should help the team decide what's coming up, what's relevant enough to acknowledge, who owns any resulting content and by when, and what has been approved, scheduled, published, or intentionally skipped.

The underlying calendar item model must be generic enough to support future programs, not just this feature. A calendar item has: title, item type (Chatter event, partner/co-hosted event, community observance, heritage/social-justice moment, winter/outdoor sports moment, content campaign, fundraiser/donation drive, partner opportunity, or content opportunity), start/end date, recurrence or annual-observance rule, time zone, summary, priority tier, calendar status, public visibility, owner, related programs, tags/categories, and audit timestamps. **Implemented** (issue #191): recurrence can optionally be structured as a fixed month-day anchor (a single day, or a month-day range) plus a `series_key` shared across a recurring observance's yearly instances, on top of the always-present free-text `recurrence_rule` description. Only fixed-date/fixed-range observances get structured recurrence; variable weekday-based rules (e.g. "the second Monday of October") or dates an operator confirms by hand each year keep free text only and are excluded from the automation below.

Two independent state machines apply per item:

- **Priority tier** — Tier 1 (Chatter should usually acknowledge or plan around this; target ~15–20/year), Tier 2 (consider when relevant to current programs/capacity/context), or Tier 3 (internal reference/content-bank only, no publication obligation). An admin can change an item's tier and must record a rationale; Tier 1 never auto-creates a publish task without a human decision.
- **Calendar status** (idea/active/complete/archived) is distinct from **content status** (not planned/idea/draft/in review/changes requested/approved/scheduled/published/skipped), since planning a moment and producing content for it are separate concerns. Every status change records the actor and timestamp; a skipped item records a reason.

Categories (LGBTQ+ community; winter and outdoor sports; community and social justice; Chatter events; campaigns and fundraising; partner opportunities) are labels layered on top of item type and priority, not a substitute for either.

**Public surface:** a Community Calendar (month/list view, filterable by month and public category) showing published Chatter events and selected public community moments — never the full internal calendar, and never internal owners, draft copy, review notes, or unpublished assets. A public item can be informational without implying Chatter hosts or owns the observance, and must degrade gracefully for long titles, missing images, date ranges, and no-match filters.

**Portal surface:** month/list/agenda calendar views with the same filter set as the item model above, plus a content-status filter that selects items holding at least one piece at that status; item CRUD (create/edit/duplicate/archive/restore) with date validation and recurrence-overlap warnings; **content pieces** (issue #1231) — one row per post or story, which may be linked to a calendar item, to an event, to both or to neither (issue #1792: a piece stands on its own, so deleting its item or event clears the link and keeps the piece and its history), each with a title, a status, an owner and reviewer, draft/review/publish due dates, internal notes, and one free-text `content` area for the angle, the channels, the call to action and whatever is still outstanding — with lead-time defaults that calculate draft/review dates from a target publish date (e.g. a 21-day lead time on a March 31 piece defaults to a March 17 draft date, March 24 review date); the Tier-1-no-decision warning; and full audit history (who changed what, approvals, publish/skip decisions) that survives archiving, following the existing `audit_log` pattern in [§5.11](audit.md#511-audit-and-history)/[§6](../technical-spec.md#6-proposed-data-model). The list and agenda views summarise an item's content with one badge: the status of its least-advanced piece still needing work, and the number of pieces once there is more than one.

**Retired, not entitlement-gated** (issue #1230): a work queue, a brief-template library with versioned field schemas, admin-configurable program-suggestion rules, related-item links and an annual planning review report were all built to this spec (issues #107, #108, #110, #111, #113) and then removed, because none of them had a settled workflow behind them and all of them cost every unrelated change a green test suite. The commit that removed them is the design document for bringing any of them back once the workflow is known. Issue #1231 then reshaped what remained: the one-to-one brief, whose eight labelled boxes asked for a structured answer to questions nobody could yet phrase, became a list of content pieces with one free-text area each — because a calendar item produces several posts or stories in practice, not one brief.

**Recurring-coverage reminder, auto-generate, and bulk import** (`/portal/calendar/import`, issue #191): a repeatable follow-on to the one-time Tier 1/Tier 2 seed below. For every structured-recurrence series, an in-app-only reminder (matching the existing notifications-menu/attention-items pattern, `content_calendar` manage-gated, surfaced from October 1 for the following year) flags Tier 1/2 series with no instance yet dated in the upcoming year. From the same page, an admin can generate a single missing series or all of them at once — each generated row copies everything a human would otherwise retype (title, type, categories, programs, source/region, sensitivity flag/tone guidance) and always lands as an internal draft (`idea` status, no decision) pending the usual review, never auto-published; re-running "generate" is a no-op once a year is covered, since missing coverage is recomputed at generation time rather than trusted from the UI. The same page also supports CSV bulk import of new, one-off external observances (distinct from the recurring-series generator): an operator enters one batch-level source (e.g. "GLAAD 2027 calendar") applied to every row plus an optional per-row region, previews per-row validation errors before submitting, and every imported row is force-set to `idea`/internal/no-decision regardless of what the CSV contained — matching the annual observance list's operations-lead-curated-internally decision (`planning/decisions/2026-08-26-content-community-calendar-open-decisions.md`): this is an import aid for a human-reviewed list, not a live external feed sync.

Editorial guardrails apply throughout (issue #113, reduced by #1230 and #1231): a calendar item can be flagged `is_sensitive_topic` (e.g. HIV/AIDS remembrance, Transgender Day of Remembrance) with tone guidance surfaced on its Content card — informational now, not a gate. The consent record and the reviewer sign-off that once hard-blocked approving/scheduling/publishing went with the brief templates that carried them. The required statement of the organization's connection went with the prose fields it guarded in #1231; what it asked for is one of the things the free-text `content` area is for, without a save-time gate in front of it. Content pieces keep their `internal_notes` field and its policy-level warning never to record specific personal, medical, legal, or confidential case details there — a policy guardrail, not automated detection.

The first-year seed is a curated Tier 1/Tier 2 set (see the planning doc's suggested list), not an exhaustive third-party awareness-day database, and requires operations and community-lead sign-off before import; each date is stored with its source, region, and any year-specific exceptions. Every subsequent year is no longer a manual one-off: the recurring-coverage reminder/auto-generate/bulk-import tooling described above (issue #191) turns the one-time seed into a repeatable process.

**Status.** Implemented: the item model, portal CRUD/filters/month-list-agenda views, content pieces with their status pipeline and lead-time scheduling, the public Community Calendar page, the Tier-1-no-decision warning, full audit history, the Tier 1/Tier 2 seed list, the sensitive-topic flag and tone guidance, and the recurring-coverage reminder/auto-generate/bulk-import tooling (`/portal/calendar/import`) — issues #103, #104, #105, #106, #109, #112, #113, #191, see [§6](../technical-spec.md#6-proposed-data-model). Retired by #1230 after shipping: the work queue (#108), the brief-template library (#107), program-suggestion rules and related-item links (#110), the annual planning review report (#111), and the consent/sign-off gates (#113). Reshaped by #1231: the one-to-one brief became many content pieces per item. Since #1794 every social post, whether related to an item, an event or nothing, is planned on `/portal/social` (a month grid and a list placed by publish date, an Unscheduled tray, filters by status, owner, reviewer and what it is related to, all kept in the URL), with a page per post at `/portal/social/[pieceId]`; it is filed under Calendar in the navigation and gated by `content_calendar` like the rest of the module. Since #1795 an event's own posts are also planned from a Social content section on its detail page (`/portal/events/<id>?tab=social`, under Overview in its rail, shown with `content_calendar: view` and edited with `content_calendar: manage`), where a post added starts related to the event with a publish date a week before it, and an overdue draft, review or publish date counts toward the section's outstanding badge. Its **Generate plan** button (#1798) previews a starter set of `idea` posts, each due a whole number of days from the event start in the tenant's `org.timezone` (Announce −30, Reminder −7, Last call −1, Day-of 0, Recap / thank-you +2 by default, held per tenant in the `content.event_plan_template` setting, with draft and review dates from the default lead time), and creates the ticked ones, skipping any title the event already has a post with. When a save moves the event's start (#1799), a content manager is asked whether to move its dated posts that are neither published nor skipped by the same amount; accepting moves their publish, review and draft dates in one statement (`shift_event_social_posts`, audited row by row). A cancelled event's section lists its open posts with a one-click **Skip all**, which records `Event cancelled` as each one's skip reason; published posts are never touched. A calendar item's Social content card links each post to its page (#1796), and its list/agenda badge and content-status filter still count only the posts related to that item. **Governance agenda feed (decided in #1796):** the Marketing & Social section's rows are calendar items, so it shows only the posts related to an item in the window; standalone and event-only posts are not added as rows of their own, since the section answers "which dates in this window still owe content", and those posts are followed on `/portal/social` and the event's section. Since #1838 a post can also be related to a program: the post form's Related to picker offers programs beside events and community dates, `/portal/social` filters by one program (`?program=<id>`), and a program's details on `/portal/programs` count its posts (with `content_calendar: view`), link to them, and offer **New post** (with `content_calendar: manage`), which opens the form on `/portal/social` already related to the program. The demo tenant's nightly seed plans a handful of posts across the status ladder — standalone, event-related, item-related and one related to both, one of them unscheduled (#1797). No post ever reaches `public_calendar_items` or the public site. The nine-value content-status pipeline is deliberately kept as it is; trimming it, if the work turns out not to need that much ceremony, is its own issue. Optional iCal export remains unbuilt (tracked as a follow-up to #111). Full requirements: `planning/ideas/content_community_calendar.md`. Delivery is planned in three phases — (1) calendar foundation: item model, categories/tiers/statuses/owners/visibility/recurrence/audit fields, portal CRUD, public list; (2) content workflow: opportunities, templates, assignment/review, my-work/overdue/history; (3) program intelligence: configurable program suggestions, related-item recommendations, annual reporting, optional iCal export — tracked as issue #102 and its sub-issues, with only iCal export of phase 3 still open. Several open questions (which roles approve public content; which channels the first brief targets; whether public community moments get detail pages; who owns the annual observance list; notification channel; iCal export phase) were resolved 2026-08-26 — see `planning/decisions/2026-08-26-content-community-calendar-open-decisions.md` — with iCal export confirmed as a Phase 3/#111-follow-up item, not pulled forward.

## 6. Data model — Content and community calendar

Data model, portal CRUD, the public surface, the content-piece workflow, audit history and the recurring-coverage reminder/auto-generate/bulk-import tooling are implemented (issues #103, #104, #105, #106, #109, #112, #113, #191, #1231). The tables behind the work queue, brief templates, program-suggestion rules, related-item links and the annual review report were dropped by #1230 and are described below only where a remaining column or view still refers to them; optional iCal export remains planned as a follow-up to #111 — see §5.20 and issue #102.

- `calendar_items`: title, item type, `starts_at`/`ends_at`, recurrence/annual-observance rule, time zone, summary, priority tier + rationale, calendar status (idea/active/complete/archived), public visibility (public/internal/unlisted draft), owner (`owner_id` → `auth.users`), `decision`/`decision_note` (plan/skip/defer, per #104 — the requirements doc's base item-management field, distinct from the content-opportunity brief owned by #106), `source`/`region`/`exceptions` (issue #112, seed provenance), `is_sensitive_topic`/`tone_guidance` (issue #113 — a sensitive-topic flag with tone guidance surfaced on the item's brief; the `sensitive_review_by`/`sensitive_review_at` sign-off pair was dropped by #1230), `series_key`/`recurrence_start_month`/`recurrence_start_day`/`recurrence_end_month`/`recurrence_end_day`/`recurrence_end_is_month_end` (issue #191 — optional structured recurrence: a month-day anchor pair grouped by `series_key` across a recurring series' yearly instances, paired-nullable with each other and independent of the always-present free-text `recurrence_rule`; `recurrence_end_is_month_end` computes the true last day of the end month at generation time rather than storing a hardcoded day, so month-end series resolve correctly in a leap year), created/updated timestamps and actor columns.
- `calendar_item_categories`: item ↔ category (lgbtq_community, winter_outdoor_sports, community_social_justice, chatter_events, campaigns_fundraising, partner_opportunities), a fixed label taxonomy, not free-text tags.
- `calendar_item_programs`: links a `calendar_items` row to one or more `programs` rows.
- Recurring-coverage reminder/auto-generate/bulk import (issue #191, `/portal/calendar/import`): app-level TypeScript, not a new RPC — every table this reads/writes is already gated by `content_calendar` for the calling user, so there's no cross-resource RLS gap to paper over. `findMissingCoverageSeries`/`getMissingCoverageSeriesForYear` group structured-recurrence Tier 1/2 items by `series_key` and flag any with no instance dated in the target year; `generateNextYearInstanceAction`/`generateMissingCalendarSeriesInstancesAction` insert the next instance (always `idea`/internal/no-decision), re-checking coverage at execution time rather than trusting the caller so re-running is a no-op once a year is covered; `bulkImportCalendarItemsAction` inserts new one-off rows from a CSV batch, force-setting the same idea/internal/no-decision state and stamping `source`/`region` regardless of what the CSV contained.
- `public_calendar_items`: curated read-only view (public + active/complete items only, no owner/internal fields), granted to `anon`, mirroring `public_events`; the editorial-guardrail columns above are internal-only and excluded from this view's explicit column list.
- RLS via the `content_calendar` resource (admin/event_coordinator manage, finance/board/volunteer view); `audit_log` covers `calendar_items`; `list_calendar_owners()` RPC (mirrors `list_event_leads()`) backs the portal's owner picker.
- `content_opportunities`: **optionally linked to a calendar item and/or an event** since issue #1792 (many per item since #1231) — one per post or story, called **content pieces** everywhere a person reads. The table kept its original name so its `audit_log` history, RLS policies and `audited_tables` registry entry carried over untouched; what #1231's migration dropped is the pair of unique constraints that made it one-to-one (the original column-level `unique` and the mirrored `(tenant_id, calendar_item_id)` unique that 20260906080000 added so PostgREST would embed the row as an object — dropping the second is what turns the embed into the array the portal reads), replaced by a plain `(tenant_id, calendar_item_id)` index. Since #1792 `calendar_item_id` is nullable and a nullable `event_id` sits beside it; both are composite `(tenant_id, …)` keys (so a piece cannot point at another tenant's item or event) with `on delete set null (<column>)`, so deleting the item or event clears the link and keeps the piece. Nothing relates the two links. Pieces are not in `event_linked_record_labels()`, so they never block deleting an event. Since #1838 a nullable `program_id` sits beside them, the same composite `(tenant_id, program_id) -> programs` key with `on delete set null (program_id)`: a piece may hold any combination of item, event and program, or none, and deleting a program clears the link and keeps the piece. Indexed on `(tenant_id, event_id)`, `(tenant_id, program_id)` and `(tenant_id, publish_due_at)`; RLS is unchanged, since it checks the `content_calendar` resource and never the parent row. Columns: `title` (not null, non-blank — what makes a list of three pieces readable) and `content` (the one free-text area, which the four dropped prose columns — `org_connection`, `recommended_formats`, `recommended_action`, `outstanding_work` — were backfilled into as labelled paragraphs), content status (not planned/idea/draft/in review/changes requested/approved/scheduled/published/skipped, check-constrained to require `skip_reason` when skipped), `internal_notes` (issue #113 — general staff working notes, carrying a policy warning to never record specific personal/medical/legal/confidential case details), owner/reviewer (`people`), configurable `lead_time_days` (org default seeded in `app_settings` as `content.default_lead_time_days`), draft/review/publish due dates (defaults computed client-side from the target publish date and lead time — draft at two-thirds, review at one-third — editable per piece), and a single `status_changed_by`/`status_changed_at` pair for the most recent transition. RLS reuses the `content_calendar` resource, same as `calendar_items`; `app_settings`' select policy was widened to include `content_calendar` managers so `event_coordinator` can read the lead-time default. Full multi-transition history is available via `audit_log` (issue #109). Nothing about a piece reaches the public Community Calendar: `public_calendar_items` is unchanged.
- **Post content parts** (issue #1826): `content_opportunities.post_format` (`instagram_post` — single image or carousel — `instagram_reel` or `instagram_story`, nullable, named with the platform so others can be added without a rename) and `collaborator_handles` (`text[]`, normalised Instagram handles, at most three and none on a story, both check-constrained). The existing `content` column is the **caption** ("Story text" on a story) — renamed in the UI only, because production runs `main` and migrations stay additive. Instagram's 2,200-character caption limit is shown as a count, not enforced, since older posts used the box for planning notes. **`social_post_tags`** holds the people to tag, in order: each row is a `person_id` (composite FK to `people`, `on delete cascade`) _or_ a bare lowercase `handle`, never both; a person's handle is never copied — it is read from `people.instagram_handle`, else their latest registration's, when shown, so a retention purge leaves nothing behind. Tags are replaced as a list by `set_social_post_tags()` (security invoker, refuses without `content_calendar` manage). `social_post_tag_people()` (invoker) lists who can be tagged with whether they declined photo consent — their answer for the post's event first, else their latest — and the sheet asks before tagging someone who declined. **`social_post_media`** holds a post's photos and videos in carousel order (`position`, unique per post, deferrable) in the private **`social-post-media`** bucket under `{tenant_id}/{post_id}/{uuid}.{ext}`; rows are written only by `record_social_post_media()` (definer, reads type and size off `storage.objects`, at most ten per post), reordered by `reorder_social_post_media()`, and deleted by the post's delete action, which removes the objects before the rows cascade. It reuses the event-media uploader (`EventMediaSlot.bucket`). All three read with `content_calendar` view and write with manage; both tables are audited. **Media from the event** (issue #1827): a post related to an event has **Choose from event** beside the uploader, a picker over that event's media (the event's flyer first) that allows only what the format takes — one video on a reel, one file on a story, up to ten otherwise, less what the post holds. A chosen item is a `social_post_media` row with `event_media_id` (composite FK to `event_media (tenant_id, id)`, `on delete set null (event_media_id)`) and no `storage_path`: the file is referenced, never copied, signed from `event-media` with the reader's own session (a photo's thumbnail where it has one). When the event media is deleted the row stays with neither, and the post shows it as **missing** until someone removes it; removing a reference never touches the event's file. References are written only by `add_social_post_event_media()` (definer: `content_calendar` manage and `events` view, every id the post's event's, repeats skipped, the same ten-file cap). Event media is not linked to the people in it, so the picker applies the Media card's photo-consent rule: it states how many registrants objected and marks photos hidden from the public site.
- **Recurring posts** (issue #1839, part of #1835): **`social_post_series`** repeats a post `weekly`, `biweekly` (every 2 weeks) or `monthly`. A series is made from a post with a publish date (`create_social_post_series()`, invoker): that post becomes its first occurrence, its publish date read in the tenant's zone is the `anchor_date` and `publish_time` (with `time_zone`), and its title, caption, format, collaborators, tags (as `jsonb`, the shape `set_social_post_tags()` takes), notes, owner, reviewer, lead time and calendar-item/event links become the template. Weekly and biweekly series repeat the anchor's weekday, monthly ones its day of month, falling back to the last day of a shorter month; the wall-clock time holds across DST. `ends_on` (optional, not before the anchor) stops it; `paused_at` holds it; `ended_at` is final. `auto_approve` (default false) makes new posts start `approved` instead of `draft` — every post is reviewed unless someone turns it on. `content_opportunities.series_id` (composite FK, `on delete set null (series_id)`) and `occurrence_date` say which series and which of its dates a post fills, unique per series, so the generator (#1840) can run twice and create nothing the second time; moving a post's publish date by hand leaves its `occurrence_date`. Changes to a series apply only to posts it hasn't created yet: **Use this post for future posts** (`update_social_post_series_template()`, invoker) copies a post into the template. The post page's **Repeats** card makes a post repeat and shows the next four dates, auto-approve, change repeat, pause/resume and end; `/portal/social` marks series posts with a repeat icon and filters on **Repeats** (`?repeats=repeating|one_off`). Read with `content_calendar` view, written with manage; audited. The dates are computed by `src/lib/social-content/series.ts`, shared with the generator.
- **Recurring post generator** (issue #1840, part of #1835): the daily Vercel cron `/api/cron/social-post-series` (`CRON_SECRET`, like the other crons) creates each active series' posts once they are inside its lead time (`publish time - lead_time_days <= now`), across every active tenant on the service-role client (`src/lib/social-content/series-generator.ts`). Paused and ended series, and series in a suspended tenant, are skipped. Each run takes the dates after `social_post_series.generated_through` (or from the anchor), up to 400 per series. A date whose publish time is still ahead becomes a post copied from the template with its publish/review/draft dates, starting as `draft`, or as `approved` when the series auto-approves. A date whose publish time has already passed (a missed run, or a pause) is not created and goes in `missed_occurrences` (newest 20), which the **Repeats** card lists as **Not created**. The writes go through `generate_social_post_series_posts()` (security definer, `service_role` only), which re-reads the series under a row lock, skips any date that already has a post (`on conflict (series_id, occurrence_date) do nothing`), drops tags naming a person who has since been deleted, and records the post in the series' tenant. A generated post has no `created_by` or `status_changed_by`, so the audit log shows it as **System**, and its `series_id` names the series.
- **Random Gear Library item series** (issue #1841, part of #1835): `social_post_series.content_source` is `template` (default) or `random_gear_item`, set on the **Repeats** card (**Each post features**). A gear series' generator run picks one item per post (`social_post_gear_pick()`, internal): candidates are the tenant's items in the public Gear Library — `public_gear_catalog`'s criteria, `status = 'available'` and `intended_use = 'gear_library'` — **with a photo** (`inventory_item_is_featurable()`). Items featured in the series' latest `recent_exclusion_count` posts (default 8, 0–52) are passed over; when every candidate was, the one featured longest ago is taken. A date with no candidate is not created and is recorded in `no_gear_occurrences` (newest 20), which the card lists as "No available gear with a photo on <date>". The pick is `content_opportunities.featured_inventory_item_id` (composite FK, `on delete set null`). The app then fills the post in (`src/lib/social-content/series-gear*.ts`): the caption is the series caption with `{item}`, `{size}`, `{details}` and `{link}` replaced, or with the item and its link added below when it uses none; the link is `<tenant site>/inventory?item=<id>`. The item's photo is copied into the post's `social-post-media` (`featured_media_id`), so later edits to the item don't change the post; a photo that can't be read leaves the post without one. The post page's **Featured gear** card shows the item, linked to its inventory page, with a warning once it no longer qualifies; until the post is published (approved or not) **Pick another** and **Choose item** swap it through `set_social_post_featured_gear()` (definer, `content_calendar` manage, refused on a published post), replacing the caption and photo and keeping the status — asking first when the caption was edited. `social_post_gear_items()` (definer, `content_calendar` view) lists the candidates and the featured item, so content managers need no inventory grant. **Use this post for future posts** on a gear post takes its item back out of the caption before copying it to the template. `generate_social_post_series_posts()` now returns the posts it created.
- Audit coverage extends the existing `audit_log` pattern ([§5.11](audit.md#511-audit-and-history)) to `calendar_items` (issue #103) and `content_opportunities` (issue #109) rather than introducing a parallel history mechanism; both are wired via the generic `audit_log_row()` trigger, and the Administration > Audit log UI's table filter/labels cover both. Since #1797 a social post's entries also name the event and calendar item it was related to at the time (the row after the change, or before it for a delete), and a **Related to** filter narrows the log to social posts for an event, for a calendar item, or standalone; a link counts if either side of the change holds it, so linking and unlinking both show. `content_permissions` was audited too until #1230 dropped the table.
