## 5.9 People directory

A person's record in the People directory (`/portal/people`) shall show that individual's full operational history across roles, not just their contact details and roles:

- Donations given, if they are a donor
- Events sponsored and sponsorship details (support type, in-kind description, contribution value), if they are a sponsor
- Volunteer activity (role types, logged hours), if they are a volunteer
- Staff assignments across events, if they are staff
- Partnerships closed as won, if they are a partner
- Gear requested and gear received, if they are a recipient

This view should read from the existing donation, event-sponsor, event-volunteer, and event-staff records rather than duplicating that history onto the `people` row.

Role membership itself follows the same principle: it is **derived, not stored**. `public.people_with_roles` (issue #624) is a `security_invoker` view carrying every `people` column plus `is_donor` / `is_sponsor` / `is_volunteer` / `is_attendee` / `is_staff` / `is_partner` / `is_recipient`, each answered at read time by the `security definer` helper `person_role_flags()` from the records that create the role — donations, monetary donations, giveaway prizes, event sponsors, event registrations, event volunteers, volunteer hours, volunteer applications, event staff, won partnership opportunities, inventory movements naming a recipient, gear requests — unioned with `person_role_tags`. The helper is definer so a role never depends on the reader's access to the evidence behind it: an event coordinator holds `people:view` and `finance:none` and must still see a donor as a donor. The view is invoker so who may see the person is still decided by the `people` select policy. Reads use the view; every write still goes to `people`, and `person_role_tags` — a staff assertion with a date and an author — is the only place a role is ever written by hand.

The seven keys are the platform's; **the words are the tenant's** (#911). Donor, sponsor, volunteer, attendee, staff, partner and recipient are nonprofit vocabulary, and an organization that sells things has customers, a studio has students and instructors, a shop has clients and suppliers. Each role's singular and plural live in one `app_settings` map, `people.role_labels`, whose shape is the registry in `src/lib/person-roles.ts`, and are read through the `tenant_person_role_labels` definer view — the audience is anyone holding `people:view`, which is wider than the `manage` permissions `app_settings`' own select policy admits. They resolve through the same `{term}` placeholder engine as the lexicon (`src/lib/lexicon.ts`), so the sidebar's People sub-items, every segment page's heading, "New X" button and empty states, the role facet, the Roles column, the badges on a profile and the aspect cards are one merged vocabulary the portal layout hands down; a tenant that sets nothing reads exactly today's words. What stays platform-owned is everything a key names: the `is_*` columns, `person_role_tags.role`, and the `/portal/donors` routes — a URL is not a label. Dropping a role a tenant has no module for is phase B of #911 and waits on #900; roles beyond the registry are phase C.

This replaced four stored boolean columns. They were written by whichever code path happened to create the relationship, so linking an existing person as a sponsor flagged nobody and removing their last sponsorship cleared nothing (issue #620); the intermediate fix, a `sync_person_role_flags()` recompute on triggers over all nine source tables, was retired along with the columns.

A person may also hold a **staff** role — someone who works events in a paid or formally-scheduled capacity, as distinct from a volunteer. Staff are drawn from the same `people` directory (a person can be both staff and a volunteer) and are assigned to individual events the same way sponsors and volunteers are: a Staff tab on the event editor links `people` rows to the event via `event_staff`, with an optional role/title and notes per assignment. Managing event staff requires the same permission as managing the rest of the event ([§5.3](access-control.md#53-authentication-and-authorization)).

A person or an organization may also hold a **partner** role. It is derived from `partnership_opportunities` and only from `stage = 'closed_won'`: that table is a pipeline, so a prospecting or negotiating row records an intention rather than a relationship, and a lost opportunity retracts the role the same way deleting a last sponsorship clears `is_sponsor`. `owner_person_id` is deliberately not part of the derivation — the owner is the internal staff or board member driving the opportunity, which says nothing about the counterparty. Partner is the second type added through the aspect registry, and the first to arrive by _moving_ an existing standalone card in: the person page's Partnerships card became the Partner aspect card, and the halves that are not the role — an organization still in the pipeline, and the internal owner — split off into a Partnership involvement card.

A person may also hold a **recipient** role (#1073). It is derived from `inventory_movements.recipient_person_id` — the authoritative record of gear leaving, whether from a public request, a staff-recorded distribution or a giveaway prize — **and** from `gear_requests.person_id`, the request header. The flag therefore reads "has asked or received" rather than "has received": a pending request is not yet a receipt, but a person with an open request is exactly who a staffer has the record open for, and the aspect card keeps requests and handovers in separate sections so neither reads as the other. A `'recipient'` tag covers gear handed over before the system existed, so nothing has to fabricate a movement to carry a claim about a person.

Two things recipient deliberately does **not** get. It has **no segment** on `/portal/people`, and it is the one role the **Roles column and the profile badges decline to name**. The other six are counterparties; a browsable list of recipients is a roster of aid beneficiaries, whose data this schema already treats as more sensitive than anyone else's — their request notes are cleared on retention, they are redacted out of snapshots, and a rider can have their profile deleted on request. The column would also leak more than the data it describes: `person_role_flags()` is definer, so the chip would be legible to every holder of `people:view` while the distributions behind it stay gated on `inventory:view`. The card is the disclosure, to a staffer who already has one person's record open for a reason.

**Implemented** (issue #626). Staff is the fifth _derived_ role rather than the `is_staff` column earlier drafts of this section described: an `event_staff` row makes someone staff, a `'staff'` value in `person_role_tags` covers the person hired before their first assignment, and both come back through `people_with_roles`. It was the first type added through the aspect registry (§C of the people-role decision record) and needed one card file, one actions entry, and one line in the registry.

### The public team page

**Implemented** (issue #1014). The public Meet the Team page (`/about/team`) can read the directory instead of the retyped `about_team.members` copy, the People twin of `layout.programs_source` ([§5.14](programs.md#514-program-management)). A tenant chooses in Website › Layout as `layout.team_source`:

- **Site Content** (the default, and what every tenant that has said nothing keeps): the `about_team.members` list, unchanged.
- **People**: the `public_team` view — one row per person the tenant has put on the page, ordered by `sort_order` (nulls last) then name, exposing the display name (`preferred_name`, else `name`), a public role, a photo link and a public biography. A tenant with nobody listed sees the `about_team.empty` copy rather than an empty grid.

Nothing on a `people` row is public on its own. The opt-in is a row in **`public_team_members`** — `person_id` (composite tenant foreign key to `people`, cascade on delete), `public_role`, `photo_url` (constrained to `^https?://` like `people.logo_url`), `bio` (blank lines separate paragraphs) and `sort_order`, one per person — added, edited and removed from the **Public team page** card on the person's record, which states the public consequence at the control. Removing the row removes the person; a merge re-points it like any other reference. The public fields live in their own table rather than as columns on `people` so public copy stays off the PII table and `people_with_roles`, the `merge_people` field allowlist and the retention purge are untouched. `public_role` is free text and deliberately separate from `board_members.role_title`: staff and volunteer leads belong on the page without being board rows, and a board title need not be the public one. Select requires `people:view` and every write `people:manage`; the table is audited, since listing a person on the website is a decision someone made. The Site Content editor labels the `about_team.members` list as not what visitors see while the page reads People, and every save or removal revalidates `/about/team` unconditionally, as the Programs actions do.

### Correcting your own contact details

**Implemented** (issue #1164, epic #1160). A person linked to a directory record — a constituent whose claim a staffer approved ([§5.23](constituent-accounts.md#523-constituent-accounts)), or any staff member, whose `people.auth_user_id` was set on day one — can correct their own contact details at `/my/details` on the public host, instead of emailing a staffer to do it. Stale contact data is why a reminder does not arrive, which is what makes this the smallest write surface in the epic and the one that pays for itself first.

**The allowlist is the design, and it lives in the database.** `people` is the PII table: it carries a legal name, an anonymity flag, a source type, staff prose, a logo and merge history, and almost none of that is the subject's to edit. A constituent may write exactly ten columns — `preferred_name`, `phone`, `pronouns`, `instagram_handle` and the six address columns (fourteen until #1746 moved the rider profile onto profile questions, which `set_my_person_answers()` writes) — and the enforcement is that those ten are the **argument list** of one `security definer` function, `set_my_contact_details()`. There is no person argument, no column argument and no field map, so the set of writable columns is fixed at migration time and no caller has a say in it. The `people update` policy still requires `people:manage`, which a constituent never holds, so there is no second path: a Server Action that sent more would not compile, and one that wrote the table directly would be refused. `name`, `is_anonymous`, `source_type`, `notes`, `logo_url`, `website`, `person_type`, `auth_user_id`, `primary_contact_person_id`, `notification_email` and the role tags are staff-only.

The obvious alternative — an update policy pinned to the caller plus a column-level `grant` — does not work here, and the reason is worth recording: `authenticated` is one database role shared by every staffer and every constituent, so narrowing the grant to this allowlist would narrow it for People › Edit too. The separation has to be in the function, not in the grant. Reading is a definer function for the mirror-image reason: `people select` requires `people:view`, which a person reading their own record has no reason to hold, so `my_contact_details()` returns the allowlist plus the pending-address state and never the confirmation token.

**Last write wins**, deliberately. Every field is sent on every save and a blank means "I have cleared this", because a person must be able to empty a field somebody else filled in. A constituent can therefore overwrite a staff correction; the audit row below is what makes that visible, and a staffer keeps the last word by editing afterwards. Refusing the write instead would mean a person who cannot fix their own phone number and no screen anywhere explaining why.

**`people.email` and the auth account's address are two things.** They can already differ — a record created by staff from a paper form and later claimed by a Google account has both — and this is now a decision rather than an accident. `people.email` is the address the organization writes to, the key #1162's claim weighed and what every duplicate check matches on. A constituent may change it, but not by saving a form: `request_my_email_change()` records the new address in `email_pending` with the SHA-256 of a one-day token, nothing reads that column, and `confirm_email_change()` promotes it only when the token sent to the new address comes back. The mechanism is #1049's, reused whole — the pending triple, the hashed token, the rate limiter, the delivery ledger and the confirm page's own component — while the promotion stays its own function, because one redirects mail and the other moves an identity key. Uniqueness is checked when the change is asked for and again when it is confirmed, which is the answer to "must not let someone edit their way into matching another record": `people_email_key` is unique on `(tenant_id, lower(email))`, so a collision is refused by the database whatever the function does, and the check is what turns a constraint violation into a sentence somebody can act on. The address that is being replaced is told, because that is the only place a change nobody asked for can still be caught. **How a person signs in is not changed here**; that belongs to the identity provider.

**Every self-edit leaves an audit row naming the constituent**, so a staffer reading a record sees the person changed it themselves rather than suspecting bad data. `people` is registered in `audited_tables` **without** an `audit_log_row` trigger: the self-edit functions write their own rows, which is a deliberate exception to §5.11's rule that coverage belongs in a trigger, because there is exactly one write path by construction and a trigger on `people` would audit something else entirely — every import, every registration upsert and every notification-token write. Reversing #18's decision to leave the PII table untriggered is a platform decision, not this ticket's. The snapshot is the allowlist plus name and email, not the whole row: staff prose and a live token are not evidence of what this person did. A save that changes nothing writes no row.

The **postal address** (`address_line1`, `address_line2`, `address_city`, `address_region`, `address_postal_code`, `address_country`) is new with this issue — the schema had no address for a person, only the shipping snapshot a single gear request carries. Flattened columns rather than `jsonb`, so the audit redaction register and the retention purge can name them; `region` and a free-text `country` rather than `state`, because a tenant is not necessarily American. Staff edit the same six on the person form, `merge_people` carries them so a merge does not drop them, all six are registered in `retention_snapshot_personal_columns`, and the `normalize_person_email()` trigger clears them whenever a row is anonymized — stated on the table rather than in the purge, so it holds for every path that anonymizes a person rather than the two that exist today.

**Out of scope, and still open:** photo upload, editing anything about another person (a household, a dependent), and deleting your own record, which is a retention/GDPR question interacting with `docs/tenants.md`'s export-and-deletion section.

### Acting from your account

**Implemented** (issue #1165, epic #1160). `/my` is not only a record. A person linked to a directory record can do three things there as themselves: register for an event, log volunteer hours, and choose which emails they get. Each has a public anonymous equivalent already, and the signed-in version differs in one way that matters more than any of the screens — **the caller never says who they are.** The person comes from `auth.uid()` and the request host, so no argument can steer a write onto someone else's record, and none of the three paths can mint a `people` row.

The first user of all three is a staff member acting as themselves: an administrator registering for their own organization's event. Their permissions change nothing about what these actions write, because none of the functions reads one. A registration by an administrator and one by a stranger differ only in which `people` row they carry.

**One gate, one place.** `my_constituent_person_id(module_key)` is the predicate every `/my` read and write opens with: the caller's own `people.id` in the tenant the request host resolves to, and only when that tenant has the constituent area and, where the action belongs to one, the owning module. `my_history_person_id()` — the gate on the history views of [§5.23](constituent-accounts.md#523-constituent-accounts) — and `my_contact_person_id()` are one-line delegations to it. The module check is in the database rather than only on the page, because a page is not what stops a request ([§7](../technical-spec.md#7-security-and-privacy)): every one of these RPCs is reachable with curl by anyone holding a session.

**Registering for an event** goes through `register_myself_for_event()`, a separate function from the anonymous `register_for_event()` rather than an extra argument on it. The two have genuinely different inputs: the anonymous one is told a name and an email and has to work out which person that is, through `resolve_or_create_person_by_email()` — which is where duplicate `people` rows come from. This one is never told. Everything else is the same, because the rest is a property of the event and not of who is asking: the same rate limit, the same published/public/deadline checks, the same `for update` lock on the event row before seats are counted, the same discount-code assignment. Name and email come off the record and are shown rather than typed; party size, phone, pronouns, Instagram handle and a note travel on the registration and are **not** written back to `people`, because `set_my_contact_details()` is the only thing that edits a person and a registration form is not it. The event page shows an existing registration instead of a second form. **Cancelling is out of scope and stated as such**: `event_registrations` has no cancelled state and no delete path anywhere in the application, staff included, so the only thing a button could do is destroy the row — taking the attendance figure and any assigned discount code with it. Changing your mind is a message to the organization until there is a model for it.

**Requesting gear** goes through `request_gear_items_as_me()` for the same reason and on the same shape (issue #1359): the anonymous `request_gear_items()` is told a name and an email and resolves a person from them; this one reads the person from the session and is told neither. Everything that belongs to the items or to the tenant's settings — the rate limit, the delivery and payment validation, the all-or-nothing row locks, the `gear_requests` header and its movements — is a single internal `create_gear_request()` both call, so the pair cannot drift. There is no per-request correction here at all: `gear_requests` has no contact column for one to travel on, so the cart shows a linked reader their own name and address and points at `/my/details` to change either. See [§5.4](inventory.md#54-inventory-and-donation-management).

**Logging your own hours** is covered in [§5.17](volunteers.md#517-volunteer-management): self-logged hours are provisional until a `volunteers:manage` holder confirms them, and they live in their own table until then.

**Notification preferences** are the same `person_notification_preferences` rows `/portal/account` edits, reached through `my_notification_preferences()` and `set_my_notification_preference()` rather than through the table. The table's policies resolve the caller through `current_tenant_id()` and `my_person_id()`, both of which read a tenant membership that the epic decided a constituent would never have; widening four policies with an `or` for one audience was the alternative, and a definer pair is where "this row is yours" is stated once. `/my` renders only the kinds whose `audience` is `constituent` — the registry's other entries are staff queues, and a volunteer shown "New volunteer applications" would have a switch that can never change what arrives. Those constituent kinds are the three receipts that were previously unswitchable (event registration, gear request and volunteer application confirmations): their audience now has an account, so they join the registry as **opt-out** — a person with no row gets them, and only an explicit `enabled = false` stops them. That `false` row is never deleted, because it is the evidence the opt-out was honoured. Every staff kind stays opt-in, so the invariant the digest relies on — no row means no email — is unchanged.

**Messaging a person is not a preference, and that asymmetry is the design** (issue #1203). A staff member can write to somebody from the record they are looking at — a gear request today, volunteer applications and contact messages next (#1204) — and that message is sent under the kind `staff_message`, which is deliberately **absent from `NOTIFICATION_KINDS`**. The absence is what makes it work: `hasOptedOut()` consults `person_notification_preferences` only for a kind carrying a registered default, so an unregistered kind is never suppressed. A reply about a request somebody made themselves is **correspondence**, not a subscription — it is the answer to a question they asked, and staff have to be able to send it to a person who has switched every receipt off. Registering the kind to "complete the set" would silently stop those answers, which is why `kinds.test.ts` asserts the absence rather than leaving it to be noticed. The org-wide email switch still governs it: a tenant that has turned outbound mail off sends nothing at all.

What such a message leaves behind is an `outbound_messages` row rather than a preference: who sent it, when, about which record, whether the provider took it, and the text — read by the managers of the record's own module, redacted from `audit_log`, and cleared two years after the send. The [inventory spec](inventory.md#54-inventory-and-donation-management) describes the first surface it appears on.

### Profile questions

**Implemented in part** (issue #1743, epic #1409; design record `planning/coven/product/2026-10-31-person-profile-questions.md`). A tenant may ask its own typed questions about a person — an experience level, a household size, a school grade — answered **once per person and kept current**, where an event's registration questions ([§5.5](events.md#55-event-management), #1501) are answered once per registration. Both use the same kinds (less `consent`, which is a standing permission with its own legal basis, #1318), options, any-of conditions, limits and editor; the rules live once in the database, in `question_definition()`, `question_answer_value()` and `question_shown()`, which `save_event_registration_questions()` and `apply_registration_answers()` also call.

**Data model.** `person_questions` (the question, with an optional per-tenant `key`, where it is asked — `ask_after_registration`, `ask_on_my_details` — and `snapshot_at_checkin`, which copies the answer at check-in, #1745), `person_question_scopes` (the programs a question is asked after registering in; none is every event) and `person_answers` (unique per person and question, `answered_via` `registration` | `self` | `staff` | `migration`, the registration it came in on, and the words as shown). All three are readable under `person_answers:view` and written only by definer functions; `person_answers` is audited, its values are registered for snapshot redaction, it never holds a person back from the purge, and a merge keeps the survivor's answer where both answered.

**Retired, never rewritten.** Editing a prompt or a label never rewrites an answer. A question with answers keeps its kind, an option somebody chose is archived rather than removed, and a question removed while answered is archived and can be restored from the editor.

**One authority.** `apply_person_answers()` decides what each form has — after registration: `ask_after_registration` and in scope for the event; `/my/details`: `ask_on_my_details`; staff: every current question — validates with the shared rules, holds non-staff to `required`, and **deletes the stored answer of any question the new answers hide**. The post-registration form is never pre-filled (the person was matched from a typed email address), so there an absent answer leaves the stored one and submitting nothing writes nothing; on the other forms an absent answer clears it. Four RPCs call it: `save_registrant_person_answers()` (anonymous; a registration under a day old is the credential), `set_my_person_answers()` (no person argument), `set_person_answers()` (`people:manage` and `person_answers:manage`) and `set_registrant_person_answers()` (the door: `events:manage` and `person_answers:manage`).

**Gate and editor.** The `person_questions` module is on for every plan; a tenant with no questions asks nobody anything. The questions are edited at People › People settings (`/portal/people/settings`, #1760; it was `/portal/people/profile-questions`, which still redirects), a settings view reached from the **Settings** button in the All people header and the command palette, under `person_answers:manage`. Since #1761 it has three tabs in `?tab=`: **Questions** lists every question (prompt, answer type, Required, where it's asked, answer count; drag or Alt+Arrow to reorder) beside one question's editor, grouped into Question, Who's asked and a collapsed Data group, with a preview of the question as asked; **Metrics** holds the figures; **Archived** the questions that can be restored. The selected question is in `?question=`, and on a phone its editor opens in a sheet. Every question still saves together through `save_person_questions()`, from a save bar shown while anything is unsaved, and closing or reloading the page with unsaved edits asks first.

**Where they are asked** (#1744). Every surface reads questions and writes answers through the shared validator and `apply_person_answers()`:

- **Registration step 2**, both forms: the questions with `ask_after_registration` in scope for the event's programs, read anonymously through `public_event_person_questions()`, which returns questions and never answers. The group is skippable; answering anything holds the shown questions to `required`. It is saved after the registration, through `save_registrant_person_answers()` with the new registration as the credential, so a refused answer never undoes a registration (the success screen says so instead). The anonymous form is **never pre-filled**; a signed-in, linked registrant's form is pre-filled from `my_person_questions()`. Anonymous answers overwrite the stored ones (last write wins).
- **`/my/details`**: the questions with `ask_on_my_details`, with the reader's own answers from `my_person_questions()`, below the contact groups and saved through `set_my_person_answers()` only when they changed. `required` binds.
- **The person record's Profile card** and **the registrant sheet** (and so the door, which opens the same sheet): every current question, read under `person_answers:view` (the sheet also needs `events:manage`, as the rider block did), edited under `person_answers:manage` through `set_person_answers()` (with `people:manage`) and `set_registrant_person_answers()` (with `events:manage`). Staff are not held to `required`. Answers show in their stored words, including answers to questions and options archived since.

The demo tenant's nightly reset seeds three example questions and a few answers.

**Check-in snapshots and figures** (#1745). Checking a registrant in copies their current answers to every current `snapshot_at_checkin` question into `person_answer_snapshots` (`registration_id`, `question_id`, `value`, `answer_text`, and `option_keys` — each chosen option's `key`, else its id — denormalized so relabelling never moves a past figure). A trigger on the same transition as the rider snapshot does it, so every check-in path is covered; a person with nothing to copy leaves earlier snapshots alone, and a re-check-in replaces them. Snapshots are readable under `person_answers:view` and registered for snapshot redaction. **Which answers a registration counts with:** its snapshots, or — only where it has none at all — the person's current answers, the all-or-nothing rule the rider columns use, so moving Chatter Snow here leaves past figures unchanged.

**Figures are rows, not code.** `person_answer_metrics` (`key`, `label`, `rule`, `question_ids`, `option_keys`, `sort_order`) is edited in a **Figures** section on People settings under `person_answers:manage`, saved by `save_person_answer_metrics()`. Two rules over option keys: `any` counts a checked-in attendee if any listed answer has one of the keys ("Beginner participants": ski or snowboard level `beginner`); `every_answered` counts one who answered at least one listed question and whose every answer has one of the keys. Chatter Snow's "First time on snow" is seeded with `any` (either level `first_time`), which is what the rider columns counted; switching it to `every_answered` is a deliberate config change, not part of the move. Both impact RPCs return `person_answer_metrics`, one `{label, count, answered}` per row — distinct people per event, summed — and the Results card and the Program Impact Report render them by label, qualified with "of N who answered" while coverage is short. The counts need only `event_impact:view` (and `programs_reports:view` for the report): a count is not an answer. A question that has snapshots or that a figure reads is archived, never deleted, when the editor leaves it out.

**Profile answers card.** On the event page, for `person_answers:view` (and the card's `events:view`), where the tenant snapshots at least one current choice question: registrations that were not cancelled, per option of each such question, read by the same snapshot-else-current rule through `get_event_answer_breakdown()`. A question whose condition names exactly one other question on the card is nested under that question's options (discipline → level), the shape of #1701's place breakdowns. Only snapshotted questions appear (open question 8).

**Chatter Snow's rider profile** (#1746) is four of these questions -- `riding_discipline` ("Do you ski or ride?", snapshotted), `ski_level` and `snowboard_level` (each shown for its discipline, required once shown, options keyed `first_time`/`beginner`/`intermediate`/`advanced` with hints, snapshotted) and `preferred_mountain` (its old mountain list, with "Other") -- and two metric rows, all seeded by `20261105120000` with option keys equal to the old column values. `park_riding` (#1748, `20261106120000`) was added after as configuration: optional, snapshotted, options `not_interested`/`curious`/`beginner`/`intermediate`/`advanced`, shown when either level is `beginner`, `intermediate` or `advanced` -- so someone whose every level is `first_time` is not asked. Its answers and check-in snapshots were copied in (`answered_via = 'migration'`). Everything that read the rider columns moved: the check-in trigger, both impact RPCs, retention, merge, deletion, `/my/details`, the registrants list (a **Profile** column), the answers export and the public API. The `people` and `*_at_event` columns stayed for one release, unread and unwritten, and were dropped in #1747 (`20261124100000`) along with the registration RPCs' ignored `p_riding_*` arguments.

**Retention, merge and deletion** (#1746). The `person_answers` retention policy (was `rider_profiles`; same 2-year inactivity rule and mode) snapshots a person's answers onto every checked-in registration that has none, then deletes the answers; anonymizing a person by any route does the same (`people_anonymized_drops_answers`). `merge_people()` keeps the survivor's answer per question, then drops any answer the result hides (`prune_hidden_person_answers()`). A deletion request goes through `delete_person_answers(person_id, reason, question_ids)` -- every answer or the named ones, snapshots first, at `people:manage` or `events:manage` -- from the person record's Profile card, recorded as a request run under Administration > Data Retention.
